## REGIME DASHBOARD

**Regime label / status:** **ACUTE STAGFLATION INTACT; DISORDERLY RISK-OFF EXPRESSION REMAINS FRACTURED.**

- **Change this week:** **unchanged overall, internally rotated.** The physical energy leg weakened as Brent and TTF fell, but both remained far above live confirmation thresholds. The hawkish-policy leg re-strengthened after Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech drove a one-day rise of more than 10 percentage points in September hike odds, lifted yields and the dollar, and hit gold and duration equities.
- **Risk tone:** mixed and macro-fragile rather than broad risk-off. S&P 500 and Nasdaq gained Monday-to-Friday, while Friday's rates shock reversed part of the AI-led rally.
- **Volatility:** elevated and event-led, not dislocated.
- **Gold vs real yields:** inverse relationship **confirmed**. Gold fell about 4.0% Monday-to-Friday as the dollar rose 0.7% and the 10-year real yield moved from about 2.38% to a Friday market proxy near 2.41%.
- **Oil vs inflation:** **diverged at the margin.** Brent fell 3.1% and improved Gulf flows reduced the immediate energy impulse, but hot PCE, Australian CPI and Warsh's reaction function kept policy inflation risk live.
- **Yen carry:** stretched, not unwinding. USD/JPY crossed back into the 160–162 warning zone, AUD/JPY rose and equities ended higher; no cross-asset carry unwind was confirmed.
- **China demand:** weakening / bifurcated. July industrial-profit growth slowed, domestic property and consumer sectors remained weak, copper held above its upside threshold on supply/tariff dynamics, and iron ore was only modestly firmer.
- **Price-confirmation score:** **5 aligned / 3 diverged / 1 too early.**
- **Coming-week bias:** precious metals downward pressure, medium confidence, unless US labour data reverses the rates-dollar move; battery/bulk mixed, medium confidence, until China PMI and Australian GDP resolve demand; energy two-sided with upside tail, medium-high confidence, until Gulf flows normalise; US equities mixed, medium confidence, with AI demand offset by the discount-rate shock; ASX mixed, medium confidence, pending GDP and China PMI; USD/front-end yields upward pressure, medium confidence, while September hike odds remain above 50%.

## THE WEEK IN 5 LINES

1. The regime did not break: Brent and TTF fell but remained far above their live physical-shock thresholds, while US 10-year yields stayed above 4.60%.
2. Warsh's Jackson Hole speech was the decisive macro event: September hike odds jumped from about 36% pre-speech to roughly 56%–58%, the 2-year yield rose 12.8 bp on Friday and gold sold off sharply.
3. AI demand still absorbed part of the shock: Nvidia reported 106% year-on-year revenue growth and Nasdaq gained 1.6% Monday-to-Friday, ending marginally above 26,400.
4. China remained bifurcated and the yen stayed vulnerable: copper held above $6.30, iron ore barely rose, industrial-profit growth slowed and USD/JPY returned above 160.
5. **Highest stakes ahead:** Friday's US payrolls report will decide whether the Warsh-driven rate-hike repricing extends toward the 75% trigger or retreats below the 50% invalidation.

### Cross-asset synthesis

The week rotated from energy-led stagflation toward policy-led stagflation. Brent's decline and slowly improving tanker flows reduced the immediate oil impulse, but the Gulf system did not normalise: Thursday traffic remained below its recent average and exports were still materially below pre-war rates. European gas stayed acutely tight as Qatari exports remained heavily impaired and storage was reported at its lowest seasonal level in 13 years. [Reuters on oil flows](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/oil-track-weekly-loss-even-iran-tensions-simmer-2026-08-28/), [Reuters on stranded Qatari gas](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/war-strands-qatari-gas-6-months-us-sales-rise-european-stocks-plummet-2026-08-26/), [Guardian on EU storage](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/aug/29/european-gas-stores-lowest-13-years-eu-price).

The rates side supplied cleaner confirmation. Core policy concern survived the energy pullback, then Warsh argued that inflation had not convincingly returned to 2% and that financial conditions were not restrictive. The dollar, front-end yields and hike odds rose together; gold and Friday's Nasdaq moved inversely. This is the live sign-flip functioning as designed: stronger or more hawkish US information was negative for gold and long-duration equities through yields.

Nvidia prevented a broad liquidation. Fiscal-Q2 revenue was $96.2 billion, up 106% year-on-year, with data-centre revenue of $89.0 billion, up 117%. Nasdaq therefore ended the week higher and just above the standing 26,400 resilience line despite Friday's reversal. ## 4. CROSS-CURRENTS TO CARRY FORWARD

1. **Energy price relief versus physical scarcity.** Brent and TTF declined, but Gulf exports remained below pre-war levels and European gas inventories remained unusually low. Lower price is not yet repaired plumbing.
2. **Warsh versus Nvidia.** The Fed reaction function raises the discount rate; AI earnings keep the numerator unusually strong. Nasdaq's two-point close above 26,400 is evidence of balance, not safety.
3. **Weak dollar level versus firmer dollar impulse.** DXY rose during the week but finished below 100. The policy leg strengthened without restoring the old strong-dollar regime pillar.
4. **BOJ hawkishness versus yen weakness.** Timely-hike language and firmer Tokyo CPI did not strengthen the yen. USD/JPY above 160 raises intervention risk, while AUD/JPY says carry has not unwound.
5. **Copper strength versus China breadth.** Copper remains above the upside threshold, but slowing industrial-profit growth, weak property demand and nearly flat iron ore deny a broad reflation call.
6. **Hot observed inflation versus lower oil.** US PCE and Australian CPI surprised upward even as crude fell. Central banks are responding to accumulated and broad inflation, not only the weekly oil impulse.
