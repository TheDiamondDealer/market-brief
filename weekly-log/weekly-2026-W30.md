## TL;DR — THE WEEK IN 5 LINES

1. The week moved from an acute oil shock into a broader stagflation trade: Brent gained about 8.5%, US10Y rose 9 bp and September Fed hike pricing reached roughly 82%.
2. Physical evidence mattered more than rhetoric—tanker attacks, near-minimal Hormuz traffic and sharply higher crude-grade premia confirmed that oil was not merely a headline spike.
3. Gold still finished about 1.1% higher after a midweek haven rally, but Thursday’s reversal restored the real-yield drag and left $4,000–$4,050 as the near-term test.
4. Nasdaq lost about 2.7% as higher discount rates met AI capex fatigue; the ASX’s earlier resource cushion failed Friday and the index ended below 8,780.
5. The highest-stakes issue next week is the Fed: an expected hold is not enough—the statement and Chair Warsh must either validate or resist September-hike repricing.

### The three themes that mattered

**1. The physical oil shock became the macro regime.** Brent settled above $100 Thursday. Tankers were attacked, Hormuz traffic remained near three daily transits, Saudi cargoes were offered through Egypt, physical North Sea and Middle Eastern premia surged, and Kazakh output was cut after Black Sea disruption. Dated Brent reached $105.70 and Forties $108.77. [Reuters physical crude](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/physical-oil-prices-jump-with-some-nearing-110-iran-ukraine-wars-hit-supply-2026-07-24/)

Friday’s peace-talk report cut Brent 3.9%. That proves oil is headline-convex, but it is not de-escalation without improved ship counts, loadings and physical premia.

**2. The inflation-policy channel finally joined oil.** US10Y rose 9 bp, real US10Y 8 bp, September Fed hike pricing reached about 82%, DXY firmed and USD/JPY ended near 164. US claims fell to 187,000, Australia added 76,300 jobs and the ECB held at 2.25% while leaving September tightening open. Strong data acted as bad news for duration because energy had raised the inflation starting point.

**3. AI earnings stopped offsetting the discount rate.** Alphabet and Tesla converted capex and cash burn into a market-wide valuation test. Alphabet lost 7%, Tesla 14.5%, and Friday Intel fell 7.9% while SOX dropped 4.5%. Nasdaq closed below 25,000. Earnings can remain strong while stocks fall if capital intensity rises faster than confidence in returns.

## FINAL FRAME

The week did not break the standing regime; it completed more of it. Oil remains the first-order variable, but the next decision point is the Fed’s reaction function. A hawkish hold can preserve acute stagflation even if Brent stays below $100. A dovish hold can reverse rates, DXY and gold without repairing physical energy flows. That separation—**physical shock versus policy multiplier**—is the central frame for the coming week.
