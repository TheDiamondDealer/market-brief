## TL;DR — THE WEEK IN 5 LINES

The AI trade broke into a broad equity and credit event: Nasdaq lost 10% Friday-to-Friday and the S&P 500 entered correction territory.
Nvidia beat and raised, but its post-earnings rally failed as leadership, accounting and financing concerns overwhelmed the headline demand signal.
China's weak October activity pulled copper, nickel, ASX materials and the Australian dollar lower, while Australian jobs data reinforced growth risk.
Treasury yields and crude fell, yet gold and silver also collapsed, showing forced de-risking and dollar pressure rather than a clean disinflation trade.
US PCE on Wednesday is the highest-stakes test next week: a hot print can restart the yield shock, while a soft print must repair credit and breadth to matter.

### The themes that mattered

1. **AI repricing crossed the equity-credit firewall.** Nvidia reported $115.8bn of revenue, up 103% y/y, and guided fourth-quarter revenue to $130bn plus or minus 3%, above consensus. Yet its shares reversed an initial rally, then fell 14% Friday as leadership, regulatory/accounting and financing concerns displaced the demand beat. The Nasdaq lost 10.0% Friday-to-Friday, all S&P sectors fell Friday, high-yield OAS widened to 3.52%, and the lowest-quality credit tier deteriorated most. That combination satisfies the live restoration rule for broad disorderly risk-off. Sources: [Nvidia results](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx); [Reuters Nvidia reaction](https://ca.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-earnings-beat-estimates-attention-turns-to-call-4965206); [FRED high-yield OAS](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLH0A0HYM2).

2. **The inflation regime persisted even as yields finally rallied in risk-off.** October retail sales rose only 0.2% and industrial production fell 0.4%, but FOMC minutes showed almost all participants expected further tightening and some discussed a larger December move. US10 fell below 5.00% only when equity liquidation accelerated; gold failed to benefit and instead fell 8.3%. This is not a clean dovish/disinflation signal—it is a collision between hawkish policy, weaker activity and liquidity stress. Sources: [US retail sales](https://www.census.gov/retail/marts/www/marts_current.pdf?release=20261117); [industrial production](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/default.htm); [FOMC minutes coverage](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Fed%2Bpolicymakers%2Bfocused%2Bon%2Binflation%2Brisks%2Bin%2BOctober%2C%2Bwith%2Bsome%2Bseeing%2Bneed%2Bfor%2Bbigger%2Brate%2Bhike/27369499.html).

3. **China and Australia added a real-economy confirmation leg.** China industrial output grew 4.8% y/y, retail sales just 0.4% and January–October fixed investment fell 8.1%, all weaker than expected. Australia wages rose 1.1% q/q and 3.5% y/y, but employment fell 43,500 and unemployment rose to 4.9%. Copper, nickel, ASX materials and AUD all weakened. The RBA's hawkish minutes therefore face a sharper growth trade-off rather than a simple inflation signal. Sources: [China activity](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/chinas-factory-output-retail-sales-growth-slow-in-october-5028313); [ABS wages](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/wage-price-index-australia/sep-2026); [ABS labour force](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/labour/employment-and-unemployment/labour-force-australia/oct-2026); [RBA minutes coverage](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/rba-board-judges-fifth-rate-hike-needed-to-provide-policy-assurance-5031363).

**Regime verdict:** the acute stagflation frame remains intact, but its expression has materially shifted. Broad disorderly risk-off is restored through equities and credit even as Treasury yields and crude de-escalate. The live state therefore needs a dated amendment, not a new baseline or threshold rewrite.

**Correlation and sign-flip changes:** Treasuries regained a safe-haven role; gold did not. The usual lower-real-yield support for precious metals failed under forced de-risking. Strong domestic wage data failed to support AUD because China/global growth and credit stress dominated. Yen carry is deteriorating, but USD/JPY's modest move means the unwind is incomplete.

## CROSS-CURRENTS

- **AI demand versus AI financing:** Nvidia's operating demand remains exceptional, but markets are discounting how the build-out is financed, the durability of margins and governance/leadership risk. That is why a beat-and-raise could coexist with a 23% weekly share decline and wider credit.
- **Treasury rally versus hawkish Fed:** a lower US10 is no longer automatically risk-positive. This week it reflected flight-to-quality while the Fed still leaned toward more hikes. A durable equity repair requires both lower yields and narrower credit, not bonds alone.
- **China weakness into Australia:** weaker Chinese investment and retail demand hit copper and ASX materials, while Australian employment contracted. The RBA is caught between 3.5% wage growth and a 4.9% unemployment rate; AUD therefore trades more like a global/China risk proxy than a pure rate differential.
- **Crude de-escalation versus gas stress:** Brent below 95 and WTI near 84 reduce the headline energy impulse, but TTF rose and the Fed remains focused on persistent inflation. The physical shock is easing unevenly rather than disappearing.
- **Gold's failed haven bid:** lower real yields, a falling equity market and higher volatility normally help gold, but gold and silver collapsed. The decisive evidence is DXY above 103 plus broad liquidation: cash/liquidity demand dominated the haven channel.

## CONVICTION SHIFTS

- **More compelling: broad disorderly risk-off.** The trigger is no longer inferential: DXY >101.5, Nasdaq <25,900, weak breadth and wider credit occurred together. Conviction is high, subject to persistence through next week's holiday-thinned tape.
- **More compelling: China/Australia downside transmission.** Weak China activity, contracting Australian employment, copper below 6.10, nickel below 16,000/t, ASX below 8,780 and AUD below 0.688 all align.
- **More compelling: precious-metals downside under liquidity stress.** Gold below 4,050 and silver's 12.2% fall show that falling real yields are not enough while DXY and forced de-risking dominate. This setup weakens only above gold 4,150 or if DXY falls below 100 with credit repair.
- **Less compelling: duration-disorder-as-rising-yields.** US10 closed below 5.00 and Treasuries rallied during the worst equity session. Duration volatility remains high, but the active expression rotated from rising yields to safe-haven demand.
- **Less compelling: crude-led stagflation acceleration.** Brent ended below 95 and WTI fell 6.2%. The regime persists through gas, wages, core inflation and Fed policy, but outright crude no longer confirms acceleration.
- **Still incomplete: yen carry unwind.** AUD/JPY weakened, yet USD/JPY only eased to 158.54 and no 3% yen surge occurred. Credit/equity deterioration raises the risk, but FX confirmation is missing.
