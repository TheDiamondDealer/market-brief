### The three themes that actually mattered

#### 1) The labour shock overruled the inflation narrative—temporarily

US payrolls fell 23,000 in July against an 80,000 consensus, June was revised to +20,000 and participation weakened. Markets immediately cut September hike odds from 67% to 44%, pushed the 10-year toward 4.64%, weakened the dollar and lifted duration, gold and the yen. This was a clean multi-asset confirmation of a dovish growth shock. The caveat is that services prices remained high and the 10-year never exited its >4.60 stress trigger.

#### 2) Energy price relief arrived faster than physical repair

Monday's US–Iran negotiation signal cut Brent sharply, but Friday's settlement at $83.55 still sat above the live $80 headline trigger. Proposed shipping rules, fees and vessel eligibility remained contested. Signal: the risk premium fell. Remaining signal: route and insurance normalisation are not yet complete. The week weakened the energy shock without ending it.

#### 3) AI/export strength and domestic weakness coexisted

China's July exports rose 23.9% y/y versus 22.2% expected; imports rose 27.5% and high-tech exports were 40.7% higher. Copper and iron-ore proxies rose modestly. But crude imports were 24.3% lower y/y and the prior week's official PMIs remained below 50. This is a K-shaped China impulse—supportive for semiconductor/AI supply chains, not a clean confirmation of property, steel or household demand.
