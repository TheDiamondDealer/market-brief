## CROSS-CURRENTS AND SIGN FLIPS

1. **Oil surged while breakevens finished flat-to-lower.** The physical shock transmitted through real yields, nominal yields and policy odds rather than a simple inflation-compensation rise.
2. **Rates rose while DXY fell.** The strong-dollar pillar did not return; yen appreciation and central-bank divergence kept the dollar below 100.
3. **Yen strengthened while equities fell weekly—but rallied Friday.** This is worsening carry health, not yet a clean liquidation signal.
4. **China PPI strengthened while copper and Dalian iron ore weakened.** Energy input inflation is not equivalent to domestic-demand recovery.
5. **Crude rallied while ASX energy and materials failed.** Equity investors discounted demand, margin and policy risks more heavily than spot commodity upside.
6. **Hot core CPI lifted hike odds, but US stocks and gold rallied Friday.** The immediate price response was a relief/positioning divergence; the weekly policy and rate signal remained hawkish.

## REGIME DECISION

**A dated live regime-state amendment is warranted and was committed with this review.**

The 31 July rule explicitly allowed the disorderly expression to re-strengthen if Brent reclaimed $100 **or** the US 10-year exceeded 4.75%. Both occurred with physical-flow confirmation; Fed hike pricing crossed its live trigger, and gold plus all three tracked equity indices weakened on the week. The new verdict is therefore:

**ACUTE STAGFLATION INTACT; DISORDERLY RISK-OFF EXPRESSION RE-STRENGTHENING, NOT FULLY RESTORED.**

“Not fully restored” is important. DXY remained below 100, USD/JPY remained below 160 and appreciated, US equities recovered sharply Friday, and broad credit deterioration could not be verified. The earlier strong-dollar and weak-yen pillars remain removed. Full restoration requires joint confirmation from a stronger dollar and weaker equity/credit breadth; de-escalation requires durable energy-flow repair, materially lower oil and yields, and easing policy pressure.
