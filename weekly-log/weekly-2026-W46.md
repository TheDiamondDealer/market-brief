## TL;DR — THE WEEK IN 5 LINES

Hot US consumer inflation pushed Treasury and real yields sharply higher, strengthened the dollar and broke gold below its live trigger.
China's producer inflation accelerated while household inflation softened, and copper, iron ore, lithium and the Australian dollar weakened together.
US equities recovered on the week, but Nvidia's leadership shock kept Nasdaq below 25,900 and exposed the AI concentration fracture.
Oil stayed near the mid-90s while gas and freight remained stressed, showing that inflation pressure has outlived the front-month crude spike.
Nvidia's results are the highest-stakes test next week: broad confirmation would repair AI breadth, while another financing or guidance shock could complete the equity-risk leg.

### The themes that mattered

1. **Inflation transmission migrated from oil into rates, freight and core goods.** US CPI rose 0.4% m/m versus 0.3% expected and core CPI 0.3% versus 0.2%. The 2-year yield jumped about 18 bp on the day, US10 finished the week at 5.154%, DXY at 103.41 and gold below 3,900. Friday's PPI was softer than expected at 0.3% m/m, but the 6.5% y/y rate, 0.5% core measure and 3.9% rise in truck freight kept the higher-for-longer channel intact.

2. **China's nominal squeeze became a cross-commodity demand signal.** October CPI slowed to 0.7% y/y and was flat m/m while PPI accelerated to 5.7% y/y, led by mining, raw materials and fuel/power costs. Copper closed below 6.10, Dalian iron ore stayed below 740, lithium remained below 150,000 and AUD/USD finished below 0.688. Unlike recent mixed weeks, the tracked China complex aligned lower.

3. **AI breadth improved while concentration risk worsened.** US equities gained Friday-to-Friday and 401 S&P constituents rose, but Nvidia lost 12.8% after Jensen Huang's resignation while the semiconductor index gained about 8%. This is not broad liquidation; it is a sharp redistribution inside the AI complex. It confirms the approved concentration fracture while withholding the credit/breadth evidence needed for full disorderly risk-off.

### Regime test

**The live regime remains intact; no `regime-state.md` amendment is justified.** Duration disorder strengthened materially, but US10 finished below the explicit 5.25 further-escalation rule. DXY and Nasdaq satisfied two legs of the full-disorder test, yet weekly breadth improved and observable credit widening was not verified. Energy also failed both decisive tests: Brent did not close below 95 with repaired flows and did not reclaim 110.

The week's important correlation changes were not new regime breaks: gold's inverse relationship with real yields re-confirmed; AUD decoupled negatively from an RBA-hike backdrop; USD/JPY ignored hawkish BOJ evidence because US rates dominated; and AI risk rotated away from Nvidia rather than liquidating the whole complex.

## CROSS-CURRENTS

- **Crude versus embedded inflation:** Brent is far below October's highs, but US PPI goods, truck freight, TTF and Henry Hub remain elevated. The front-month crude price now understates the inflation impulse already embedded in logistics and core goods.
- **China producer/consumer split:** PPI at 5.7% and CPI at 0.7% describe a margin squeeze rather than healthy reflation. Monday's activity data must show whether export-oriented production can keep offsetting weak retail/property demand.
- **BOJ versus Fed dominance:** Japan's CGPI and meeting summary supported faster hikes, but US yields still drove USD/JPY to 160. The next risk is non-linear intervention, not a smooth rate-convergence trade.
- **AI concentration versus breadth:** the Nvidia shock was severe, but AMD/Broadcom and the wider chip index rose. Next week's Nvidia results will test whether this is company-specific leadership risk or an industry financing/capex problem.
- **Australia's split signal:** ASX remained resilient above 8,830 while AUD broke lower. Domestic wages/jobs can support RBA pricing, but China activity and the global dollar cycle still dominate the currency.

## CONVICTION SHIFTS

- **More compelling: gold downside through the real-yield/dollar channel.** The live sub-4,050 trigger is now confirmed by DXY above 101.5, US10 above 5% and real yields at 2.91%. Reclaiming 4,150 is the explicit invalidation.
- **More compelling: broad China-demand deterioration.** Copper below 6.10, iron ore below 740, lithium below 150,000 and AUD below 0.688 aligned with the CPI/PPI margin squeeze. Monday's activity data can confirm or challenge the shift.
- **More compelling: duration disorder, but not a new regime amendment.** US10 moved back above 5.00 and DXY above 103, yet the 5.25 escalation rule was not met and equities/credit did not jointly deteriorate.
- **More compelling: yen intervention risk; less compelling: orderly BOJ-led yen strength.** Hawkish BOJ evidence failed to strengthen the yen. A close above 162 without intervention would escalate; below 160 keeps the formal signal invalidated.
- **More nuanced: AI concentration fracture.** Nvidia's collapse matters, but positive broad equity breadth and strong performance elsewhere in semiconductors argue against treating it as systemic before next week's results.
- **Less compelling: outright crude as the sole inflation gauge.** Gas, freight, core producer goods and the dollar/rates complex are carrying more of the active stagflation signal.
