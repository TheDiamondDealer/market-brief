### The themes that mattered

#### 1. Physical energy escalation reasserted the acute stagflation leg

Brent climbed to $94.39 and WTI to $87.06 as only seven ships crossed Hormuz on Thursday — about half the previous day’s count — while producer curtailments and impaired routes persisted. The route handled roughly one-fifth of global oil and LNG before the war. TTF rose 6.6% Monday-to-Friday. This was verified physical impairment, not a paper-only risk premium, and it outweighed the softer demand signal from China. [Reuters oil and shipping evidence](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/oil-set-second-weekly-rise-unsettled-us-iran-war-crimps-supply-2026-08-21/)

#### 2. Treasury intervention split the dollar from the long end and created a gold/debasement trade

Long yields remained stressed — the 10-year ended near 4.73% and the 30-year near 5.27% — even after Treasury expanded long-duration buybacks. DXY fell almost 1% for the week and precious metals surged. The durable signal is not monetary easing: it is fiscal/market-function support lowering the dollar while failing to remove term premium. That makes gold’s rally a debasement/fiscal-hedge expression, not the standing haven override, which requires gold to rise with DXY and yields. [Global rates, dollar and Treasury buyback reaction](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-08-21/) [Gold reaction](https://www.reuters.com/business/gold-steadies-heads-third-straight-weekly-gain-2026-08-21/)

#### 3. Growth diverged by region and sector

China disappointed across industrial output, retail sales and fixed-asset investment, then left the one- and five-year LPRs unchanged at 3.00% and 3.50%. Australia’s wages slowed to 3.2% y/y and employment unexpectedly fell 15,800, lifting unemployment to 4.5%. In contrast, the US flash services PMI jumped to 56.8 and the composite to 56.0, while manufacturing slowed to 53.2 amid supply disruption. The result was a two-speed macro tape: weaker China/Australia domestic demand against strong US services, all underneath an energy-driven inflation risk. [China activity](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-july-industrial-output-grew-45-yy-retail-sales-up-06-2026-08-17/) [China LPR](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/china-leaves-loan-rates-steady-15th-consecutive-month-august-2026-08-20/) [Australia wages](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/wage-price-index-australia) [Australia labour](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/australia-jobless-rate-hits-near-five-year-high-45-july-2026-08-20/) [US PMIs](https://www.reuters.com/business/us-service-sector-fuels-acceleration-business-activity-sp-global-says-2026-08-21/)

Expanded buybacks weakened the dollar and accelerated gold, but did not sustainably lower the long end. This is the week’s most important relationship shift: intervention improved market function while leaving the underlying term-premium problem intact.

### Oil → inflation → RBA/Fed reaction functions

Australian wages and jobs softened and US survey-price pressures eased, but oil and gas rose. Central banks are therefore seeing weaker domestic pressure alongside a renewed imported inflation impulse. That is precisely the policy trade-off embedded in the live stagflation regime.

### AI concentration → Nasdaq + metals

Nasdaq fell 1.7% and lost its >26,400 resilience line ahead of Nvidia earnings, while copper stayed above $6.30. The AI capex story has not broken, but equity concentration is now a higher-beta transmission channel for long-yield stress.

### More compelling

### Less compelling

- **A one-way strong-dollar/weak-yen disorderly regime.** DXY <100, USD/JPY <160, AUD/USD >0.700 and AUD/JPY higher all reject it.
- **A September Fed hike as the base case.** Pricing stayed near one-third despite hawkish minutes and strong services.
- **A broad China reflation trade.** Activity missed, LPRs were unchanged and iron ore stayed below CNY740; metals strength was asset-specific.
- **A clean disinflation conclusion from softer US price surveys.** Brent, TTF and the long end moved the other way, preserving the imported-inflation and term-premium risks.
