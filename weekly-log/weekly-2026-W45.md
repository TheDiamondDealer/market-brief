### The themes that mattered

1. **De-escalation was priced before it was physically verified.** Brent and WTI fell hardest early in the week as markets inferred that Democratic control of Congress would constrain further US escalation and improve the odds of an Iran agreement. The price signal was strong; the physical signal was not. Vessel attacks continued, a 5 November explosion killed a seafarer, and Middle East–China VLCC freight remained near $1.41 million per day. The correct interpretation is a weaker war premium, not repaired flows.

2. **Policy divergence moved through currencies rather than simple rate differentials.** The RBA raised the cash rate 25 bp to 4.85% on 3 November, but the 6–3 vote and removal of language pointing to another increase softened the path. AUD/USD fell below 0.688. On Friday, US payrolls rose 165,000, unemployment held at 4.1%, and prior months were revised up by 84,000; December Fed-hike odds moved to roughly 80–81%, DXY rose and gold reversed. The live higher-for-longer sign-flip therefore remains active.

3. **The tech liquidation was real, but broad disorder is still unproven.** Democratic midterm wins increased the perceived risk of tighter AI regulation as investors were already questioning capex returns. On Thursday every S&P sector fell, Nasdaq lost 5.5%, SOX 12% and roughly $2.5 trillion of Nasdaq value was erased. Friday's rebound prevented a second liquidation session, and observable credit/carry deterioration was insufficient to declare the live broad-disorder condition fully restored.

### Biggest winners and losers

- **Winners:** platinum (+6.4% Mon–Fri), palladium (+6.1%), gold (+3.2%), silver (+3.0%) and ASX 200 (+1.9%).
- **Losers:** TTF (-17.0% Fri/Fri), Brent (-16.4% Fri/Fri), WTI (-12.8% Fri/Fri), Nasdaq (-2.3% Mon–Fri; -5.5% Thursday) and S&P 500 (-2.2% Mon–Fri). Semiconductor equities were the acute stress pocket, with SOX down 12% on Thursday.

### Regime test

**The live regime remains intact; no `regime-state.md` amendment is justified.** The energy/physical leg weakened, but Brent did not finish below 95 with verified flow repair, US10 did not break below 4.75, DXY stayed above 101.5 and Nasdaq stayed below 25,900. Conversely, full disorderly risk-off still lacks the required multi-session equity/credit/carry deterioration. This is a material change in expression, not yet a regime break.

Correlation changes were revealing: gold rose with lower real yields through most of the week and reversed when Friday's dollar/yield impulse returned; ASX decoupled from Nasdaq; copper diverged from iron ore; and AUD fell despite an RBA hike. These are active sign-flips already permitted by the approved frame, not new ones.

## CROSS-CURRENTS

- **Energy price versus physical logistics:** lower outright crude alongside record-like tanker freight is the week's central inconsistency. It argues against treating spot de-escalation as durable until flows, insurance and freight confirm it.
- **RBA versus global dollar cycle:** domestic tightening could not support AUD because the expected terminal path softened and global USD/risk pressure strengthened. AUD/USD below 0.688 and AUD/JPY near 108.7 now transmit the external shock into Australian conditions.
- **China metals split:** copper and lithium point to weak demand; iron ore and nickel reflect steel-specific and Indonesian supply factors. Monday's China inflation and subsequent lending data must resolve whether this is demand stabilization or supply noise.
- **Japan carry:** USD/JPY below 160–162 prevented escalation, but AUD/JPY weakness shows carry is becoming less healthy at the margin. A simultaneous yen rise, equity fall and credit widening would be the decisive unwind pattern.
- **AI concentration versus haven demand:** precious metals gained while long-duration tech sold off. Gold's move remains rate-consistent rather than a clean safe-haven override because 4,150 has not held for two sessions with Brent, DXY and US10 all rising.

## CONVICTION SHIFTS

- **More compelling: energy de-escalation as a price trend, less compelling as a structural call.** The weekly decline is decisive, but the lack of freight and security repair caps confidence. Below 95 Brent with verified flow normalization would materially strengthen the structural case.
- **More compelling: short-duration/anti-concentration equity analysis.** Nasdaq's threshold breach, the all-sector Thursday decline and SOX collapse confirmed fragility. Confidence stops short of a full-disorder call because Friday rebounded and credit/carry confirmation is incomplete.
- **More compelling: dollar pressure against AUD.** AUD/USD moved from near 0.700 to below the live 0.688 threshold despite an RBA hike, with DXY and commodities confirming. A reclaim of 0.700 would invalidate this shift.
- **More compelling: precious metals as rates-sensitive havens, not yet independent safe havens.** Gold, silver and PGM gains were strong, and Friday's response to payrolls confirmed the real-yield/dollar mechanism. The independent safe-haven override still requires gold above 4,150 for two sessions while Brent, DXY and US10 all rise.
- **Less compelling: a single China-demand verdict.** Copper/lithium weakness and iron ore/nickel resilience conflict. China inflation, trade and lending data plus the 6.10/6.30 copper zone must resolve it.
- **Unchanged: no live regime rewrite.** The week altered the expression and raised the probability of broad disorder, but neither de-escalation nor full disorder met the approved evidence standard.
