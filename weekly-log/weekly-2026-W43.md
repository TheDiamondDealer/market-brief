### Regime decision

**No live regime-state amendment is warranted.** The 25 September state already classifies acute stagflation as intact, duration disorder as escalated and broad disorderly risk-off as incomplete. W43 strengthened the physical shock enough to meet the explicit Brent-above-$110 escalation condition and moved Nasdaq to the brink of 25,900, while Meta supplied the clearest credit warning to date. But the US 10-year and real yield fell, DXY softened, USD/JPY stayed below 160, the S&P decline remained below 1% Monday-to-Friday and broad credit/carry liquidation was not verified.

**Full disorderly risk-off remains conditional on** Nasdaq closing below 25,900 with weak breadth and observable broad credit widening while DXY holds above 101.5, or an equivalent multi-session equity/credit/carry liquidation cluster. A Friday close in 10-year yields below 5.00% is only the first de-escalation step; the live 4.75% condition remains unmet.

## CROSS-CURRENTS

1. **Oil rose while breakevens and real yields fell.** This is not benign disinflation. It shows that AI-financing/growth anxiety was strong enough to overwhelm the first-order energy-inflation trade in bonds. If the Fed or PCE reasserts the inflation channel, duration can sell off from a fragile starting point.
2. **Gold lost both ways.** It fell when yields were high early in the week and failed to recover when real yields collapsed Thursday. The active sign-flip still favours the policy/rates channel, but positioning/liquidity and the stronger dollar now explain more than the simple daily inverse real-yield relationship.
3. **AI concentration is spreading into funding, but breadth still blocks the “systemic” label.** Meta’s CDS and equity reaction are material; the Russell 2000 nevertheless gained 2% Friday-to-Friday and the S&P 500 lost only 0.5% on that basis.
4. **Australia became more hawkish while its resource complex weakened.** AUD strength reflected domestic rate repricing, not better China demand. That disconnect raises the risk that tighter domestic policy and weak materials earnings hit the ASX simultaneously.
5. **China’s yuan strength is not commodity reflation.** Strong exports, a firm currency and above-consensus GDP coexist with weak household demand, property investment and metals breadth. Require actual fiscal/credit delivery and same-direction copper/iron ore/AUD confirmation.
6. **Japan’s inflation signal strengthened without a carry accident.** National core CPI returned above 2% and USD/JPY fell, but AUD/JPY and broader risk did not confirm forced yen-funded deleveraging.
