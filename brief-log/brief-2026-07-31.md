## LISTEN

1. **The yen moved first and violently.** USD/JPY fell as much as 3% from above 162 and closed near 159.23 in what market participants and Nikkei said was likely official intervention. **So what:** the weak-yen trigger is invalidated, but BOJ communication today must hold the repair; this was not a benign carry unwind because US equities rallied.
2. **Soft US inflation and weak headline growth broke the dollar—not the long end.** Q2 GDP grew 1.5% annualised versus 2.1% consensus; June headline PCE fell 0.1% m/m and core rose 0.1%. **So what:** September hike odds eased to about 61–64% and DXY broke below 100, but 3.9% private domestic demand and 3.2% consumption kept the 10- and 30-year yields elevated.
3. **Microsoft overpowered the macro tape.** Its 15.5% rally added about $450 billion in market value and helped lift the S&P 500 1.7% and Nasdaq 2.8%. **So what:** AI monetisation can produce violent index repair even while the Nasdaq's standing downside trigger remains active.
4. **The AI cash-conversion split widened after hours.** Amazon rose about 8% as AWS grew 37%; Apple fell 7.8% after services missed and current-quarter guidance lagged expectations. **So what:** strong product revenue is no longer enough—the market is separating cloud growth, capex, cash burn and forward guidance company by company.
5. **Crude cooled, but products and routes did not normalise.** Brent fell to $89.03 on maritime-defence and diplomatic hopes, while attacks widened and refinery outages kept diesel, gasoline and jet cracks near records. **So what:** the market is pricing neither full escalation nor safe passage; the physical shock remains live below the headline crude price.
