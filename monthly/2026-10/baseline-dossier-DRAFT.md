## Executive frame

The durable frame remains a four-way interaction among physical commodity constraints, policy reaction functions, China demand, and financial-market transmission. September strengthened the first two and made duration disorder a primary risk. It did not produce a uniform liquidation: AI-related earnings and investment supported cap-weighted US equities, the yen strengthened after BOJ tightening, and China remained split between technology/industrial activity and property/household weakness.

The proposed current regime is **acute energy and long-duration stagflation with confirmed duration disorder, but without fully restored broad disorderly risk-off**. In daily work, separate:

1. **Physical facts** — export volumes, route capacity, inventories, spreads, refinery runs, power availability and mine output.
2. **Policy facts** — decisions, votes, guidance and market-implied probabilities.
3. **Price confirmation** — closes, breadth, credit, volatility and cross-asset co-movement.
4. **Narrative** — useful only after the first three agree.

## Part 1 — System map: what moves what

### 1. Rates, dollar and liquidity

- **Nominal yield = expected policy path + expected inflation + real term premium.** September showed why decomposing the move matters: the US 10-year rose 53 bp to 5.292%, while the 10-year real yield rose roughly 49 bp to 2.93% and the 10-year breakeven stayed near 2.35%. The dominant shock was real yields/term premium, not an unanchored inflation expectation.
- **Real yields are the first tactical filter for precious metals and long-duration equities.** Higher real yields raise the opportunity cost of non-yielding bullion and compress distant cash flows. A geopolitical headline does not override this mechanism unless price proves it.
- **The dollar is a transmission amplifier, not a sufficient regime signal.** A broad DXY rise tightens global dollar funding and pressures dollar-priced commodities, but DXY composition makes EUR moves important. Require breadth across AUD, CNY, JPY and risk assets.
- **Front end and long end can diverge.** Softer August PCE lowered October Fed-hike odds to about 37%, yet long yields finished the month at multi-decade highs. Fiscal supply, term premium and strong AI investment can keep duration under pressure even if the next meeting is a pause.
- **Credit completes a disorderly-risk-off call.** Equity weakness plus high yields is not enough; look for wider investment-grade/high-yield spreads, funding stress or disorderly volatility.

### 2. China demand chain

- **Property/steel:** housing starts, sales, completions, financing, steel margins, blast-furnace utilisation, hot-metal output and port stocks lead iron ore and coking coal.
- **Grid/technology/manufacturing:** grid investment, renewables, electronics, AI infrastructure, exports and equipment manufacturing can support copper and selected transition materials even while property is weak.
- **Credit transmission:** aggregate financing and targeted relending matter only when borrowing, sales, construction or inventories respond. A policy announcement is not demand confirmation.
- **Currency:** CNY stability can reduce imported stress and support regional FX, but a firm yuan ahead of a summit may be administrative rather than cyclical.
- **Current split:** September official manufacturing PMI rose to 50.1, production 51.7 and new orders 50.5; employment remained 48.4. Non-manufacturing was 50.2, but new orders were 46.5 and employment 45.9. Treat this as a technology/industrial improvement inside a weak domestic-demand structure.

### 3. Energy-to-inflation chain

`Physical disruption → crude/gas/product prices → transport and producer prices → inflation expectations/policy → real yields, FX and risk assets.`

- **Crude headline:** Brent/WTI show global barrel scarcity but contract rolls and quality/location spreads matter.
- **Products:** diesel, gasoline and jet cracks can sustain consumer inflation even when crude falls; refinery outages and export restrictions are independent variables.
- **Gas:** Henry Hub reflects North American production, storage and weather; TTF reflects European storage, LNG deliverability and geopolitics. Their divergence is structurally meaningful.
- **Physical confirmation:** tanker traffic, pipeline capacity, loading programmes, inventories, time spreads and refinery runs outrank statements.
- **Policy response:** central banks react differently to a supply shock. The Fed, RBA and BOJ tightened in September; the PBOC remained targeted because domestic demand is weak.

### 4. Supply concentration and policy risk

- **Nickel:** Indonesia produces more than 60% of global output and controls ore, quota and processing policy. Quotas matter only through realised ore supply, plant utilisation and exchange inventories.
- **Iron ore:** Australia and Brazil dominate seaborne supply; Simandou is becoming an operating source of high-grade ore and bargaining power for China.
- **Copper:** mine disruptions and declining grades combine with grid/AI demand. LME/COMEX/ShFE dislocations can dominate spot pricing.
- **Uranium:** utility contracting, conversion/enrichment constraints and producer guidance matter more than a thin weekly spot print.
- **Precious metals:** South African/Russian PGM supply, recycling and auto substitution drive platinum/palladium; official-sector buying is central for gold.
- **Energy:** OPEC+ headline targets, sanctions, war, route access and spare capacity interact. Deliverability is the binding question in an impaired Gulf system.

## Part 2 — Asset mechanisms, benchmarks and market structure

### Precious metals

**Gold**

- Primary daily variables: US 10-year real yield, DXY, ETF flows, futures positioning, official purchases and Asian physical premiums.
- Current tactical relationship: inverse to real yields/DXY is restored. September gold fell 6.6% as real yields surged.
- Structural support: central banks reported 23 t of net purchases in July; China added about 20 t in August. This is a medium-term floor, not a guarantee against drawdowns.
- Use spot for the headline. Do not mix spot and futures settlement without labelling.

**Silver**

- Hybrid precious/industrial asset; monitor gold direction, solar/electronics demand, ETF/coin flows, lease rates and inventories.
- The Silver Institute expects a sixth consecutive annual deficit in 2026, but high prices can destroy demand and attract recycling.
- Higher beta means it can underperform gold during real-yield shocks even with a structural deficit.

**Platinum and palladium**

- Monitor autocatalyst demand, substitution, South African/Russian supply, recycling and investment flows.
- WPIC's Q2 update moved 2026 from a 297 koz deficit to a 265 koz surplus, largely because of H1 disinvestment and weaker jewellery demand. Above-ground stocks remain depleted after prior deficits.
- Palladium remains the more auto-cycle-sensitive and higher-volatility leg.

### Battery and industrial minerals

**Copper**

- Use one benchmark consistently; COMEX can diverge from LME on tariff/location stress.
- Structural demand: grids, renewables, EVs, data centres and AI infrastructure. Cyclical demand: China construction/manufacturing.
- Confirm rallies with Chinese premiums, bonded stocks, imports and exchange inventories; a high futures price alone is not broad China reflation.

**Lithium**

- No single global price. State product, grade, geography and currency.
- Key variables: brine/spodumene output, mine curtailments/restarts, cathode inventories, EV/storage demand and Chinese conversion margins.
- September's CNY122,800/t provider series and roughly 22.5% monthly fall show that the summer rebound did not establish durable scarcity.

**Nickel**

- Stainless steel dominates demand; batteries are important at the margin. NPI/matte/class-1 distinctions matter.
- Indonesia's initial 2026 quota restraint briefly supported price, but supplemental quotas, imports and exchange stocks weakened credibility.
- Confirm an upside break with ore availability and inventory draws; otherwise treat it as policy headline risk.

**Uranium**

- Spot is thin. Use utility contracting, term prices, enrichment/conversion, producer guidance and restarts.
- The ~$89.50/lb spot indication remains above the prior $88 headline boundary; a durable bull signal requires term-market confirmation.

### Bulk commodities

**Iron ore**

- Benchmark carefully: 62% CFR China, SGX and Dalian are related but not interchangeable.
- Demand: steel margins, hot metal, construction, manufacturing and exports. Supply: Australia/Brazil weather and operations, Simandou ramp and port arrivals.
- Simandou's 2026 sales guidance is 5–10 Mt on a 100% basis; full project capacity is much larger, but near-term price impact depends on execution and blend acceptance.

**Thermal coal**

- Regional qualities and indices differ. Asia's seaborne market is shrinking slowly as renewables/domestic output rise, while Indonesian supply policy and industrial power demand can tighten exports.
- Gas prices, weather, power-sector demand and logistics drive short-run moves.

**Coking coal**

- Premium hard coking coal is tied to blast-furnace steel, Australian supply and Chinese/Indian procurement.
- Do not fabricate a threshold without a reliable, repeatable free benchmark. Keep `N/V` where comparability fails.

### Energy

**Brent and WTI**

- Always state contract month near expiry. On 30 September, expiring Brent November settled $103.50 while active December was $98.03.
- Confirmation stack: exports/loadings, tanker traffic, pipelines, inventories, time spreads, cracks and refinery runs.
- OPEC+ completed the rollback of 1.65 mb/d of cuts in September but retains roughly 2 mb/d of cuts through year-end. Actual Gulf output has been below headline quotas.

**Henry Hub and TTF**

- Henry Hub: US production, storage, weather and LNG feedgas.
- TTF: European storage, LNG arrivals, Qatar/Gulf flows, Norwegian outages and Asian competition.
- September ended near $3/mmBtu for Henry Hub and €72/MWh for TTF. The regional gap remains a material inflation and competitiveness signal.

### Equities

**US**

- Separate cap-weighted S&P/Nasdaq from equal-weight breadth, semiconductors, software and credit.
- AI investment can lift nominal growth and earnings while also pushing long yields higher. This creates a self-offsetting but fragile regime.
- A Nasdaq high is not broad risk-on if most constituents, small caps and credit disagree.

**Australia**

- Banks transmit RBA/housing stress; materials and energy transmit China/commodity terms.
- The ASX 200 fell roughly 3.2% in September even as oil rose. Materials/energy did not provide a clean cushion, while the RBA moved to 4.60% and national home values fell again.
- Require sector breadth before treating an index break as durable.

## Part 3 — Regional policy and transmission

### United States

- The Fed raised the target range 25 bp to 3.75–4.00% on 16 September, unanimously.
- August PCE was softer than expected: headline +0.3% m/m and 3.4% y/y; core 3.0% y/y. Methodology changes contributed to downward revisions.
- Q2 GDP was revised to 2.2% annualised and consumer spending to 3.8%, with AI infrastructure investment an important support.
- October hike odds ended near 37%, but December tightening remained heavily priced. Treat the meeting path and long-end term premium separately.

### Australia

- The RBA raised the cash rate 25 bp to 4.60% on 29 September, unanimously, its fourth 2026 increase.
- August CPI rose 0.4% m/m and 4.0% y/y; trimmed mean rose 0.2% m/m and stayed 3.6% y/y. Fuel jumped 14.8% m/m and housing inflation was 5.7% y/y.
- Housing is a counterforce: national prices fell 1.1% in September, the sixth monthly fall, and sales weakened.
- September-quarter CPI on 28 October is the next decisive domestic input. Energy pass-through and services/core breadth matter more than a single monthly headline.

### China

- One-year and five-year LPRs stayed 3.00%/3.50% for a 16th month in September.
- Policy remains targeted: lower-cost policy-bank funding, technology relending and first-home subsidies rather than a broad rate-cut cycle.
- The Trump–Xi summit extended the trade truce two months and created AI/military dialogue channels, but produced no major economic breakthrough.
- The core split is unchanged: high-tech manufacturing/exports can hold up while consumption, property and employment lag.

### Japan

- The BOJ raised its policy rate from 1.00% to 1.25% on 18 September by 7–2, the highest in 31 years.
- Energy import inflation and a desire to prevent overshoot shifted the reaction function more hawkish.
- Yen strength after a hike is not automatically risk-off. A carry unwind requires simultaneous equity declines, credit widening and cross-currency deleveraging.
- The 29–30 October meeting and Outlook Report are the next reset.

### Dominant live narratives

1. **Duration disorder is now independent of the next Fed meeting.** Softer PCE reduced October hike odds, but long yields rose on growth, supply and real term premium.
2. **Physical energy stress is partially repaired, not resolved.** Exports recovered late month, yet Brent remains high and peace talks stalled.
3. **AI is both hedge and hazard.** It supports earnings/capex and Nasdaq, but strengthens nominal growth, power demand and funding needs.
4. **Gold's haven override failed tactically.** Real yields and DXY again dominate daily direction; official demand is a structural support.
5. **China is bifurcated.** Technology/manufacturing stabilised; property, employment and steel-linked demand have not.
6. **Australia is a stagflation transmission point.** RBA tightening, 4% headline CPI, weak housing and softer ASX coexist.

### Live cross-region threads

- Gulf export/product flows → Brent/TTF → CPI and central-bank reaction → real yields/DXY → gold and duration equities.
- AI capex → copper/power demand and GDP → higher long yields, while AI earnings support Nasdaq.
- China targeted support → copper/technology more than iron ore/property → AUD and ASX materials remain incomplete confirmations.
- BOJ normalisation/intervention risk → stronger yen → lower AUD/JPY, but only a credit/equity cluster turns that into a carry-unwind regime.
- Simandou and Indonesian quota policy reshape commodity cost curves over quarters, not single sessions.

## Part 5 — Monitoring hierarchy and source book

### What leads the daily brief

1. Confirmed regime break or invalidation.
2. Physical-flow change with price confirmation.
3. Policy decision or material repricing with cross-asset confirmation.
4. Structural commodity change with verified source.
5. Company/sector event only when it changes a tracked macro relationship.

### Primary and authoritative sources

- Central banks: Federal Reserve, RBA, BOJ, PBOC.
- Statistics: BLS, BEA, US Treasury/FRED, ABS, China NBS.
- Energy: IEA, EIA, OPEC, national energy ministries and port/pipeline data.
- Metals: World Gold Council, WPIC, Silver Institute, ICSG, company operating reports.
- Market closes: exchange/operator data, Reuters/AP, then clearly labelled provider series.

### Research discipline

- Every quantitative claim needs a source and as-of date.
- Mark unavailable or non-comparable values `N/V`; do not interpolate silently.
- Distinguish spot, futures, active contract and expiring contract.
- Separate a price break from its physical or breadth confirmation.
- Make inference explicit; do not present it as reported fact.
- Preserve live files until the user explicitly approves promotion.
