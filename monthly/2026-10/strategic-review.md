## What changed this month — one page

### Strategic verdict

**SHIFTING — acute energy and long-duration stagflation remain intact; duration disorder is now confirmed, while broad disorderly risk-off is still not fully restored.** September replaced August's managed, divergent expression with a stronger physical-energy/policy/real-yield shock. Brent gained about 14%; the US 10-year yield rose 53 bp to 5.292%; the 10-year real yield rose from 2.44% on 1 September to about 2.93% on 30 September; the Fed, BOJ and RBA all tightened. Yet the Nasdaq gained 1.86% for the month, credit liquidation was not verified, USD/JPY finished below its old stress trigger and copper held near record territory. The regime is therefore more dangerous and broader than a month ago, but not a one-way liquidation.

**Exactly what would flip this verdict:**

- **To fully restored disorderly risk-off:** DXY closes above 102 while Nasdaq closes below 25,800 on weak breadth and credit widens, or an equivalent multi-session equity/credit/carry liquidation cluster develops.
- **To a more acute stagflation escalation:** Brent closes above $110 with verified export/product impairment, or the US 10-year sustains above 5.50% with real yields above 3.00%.
- **Toward stabilisation:** active-month Brent closes below $95 and then $90 with verified Gulf-flow repair, US 10-year closes below 5.00% and then 4.80%, DXY falls below 100.5, and equity breadth improves.

### Defining narrative

September was the month the shock migrated from commodities into duration. Oil and European gas remained high, but the important change was the 53 bp rise in the US 10-year and a roughly 49 bp rise in the 10-year real yield. That combination broke gold's August resilience and tightened global financial conditions even as AI-linked equities held up. Synchronous rate hikes by the Fed, BOJ and RBA confirmed that energy and domestic-capacity risks were not merely market rhetoric.

The cross-asset signal was still not uniform. The Nasdaq rose, the dollar stopped short of a decisive stress break, and the yen strengthened after BOJ normalisation. China manufacturing returned barely to expansion and copper stayed elevated, but iron ore, lithium, employment sub-indices and property demand remained weak. The result is an acute stagflation regime with confirmed duration disorder and unusually persistent regional/sector divergence.

## Structural shifts during September

### Simandou: ramp is supply, not a future headline

Rio Tinto's H1 operating data showed 2.2 Mt of 100%-basis Simandou sales to China and retained 5–10 Mt 2026 sales guidance. At full scale the two-mine system targets up to 120 Mtpa. The near-term constraint is execution: temporary crushing reliability, permanent-facility delivery and the two-to-three-month mine-gate-to-sale lag. Treat high-grade arrivals as a widening discount/cost-curve force, not an immediate collapse in total seaborne prices.

### Indonesian nickel: quota restraint has not earned credibility

Indonesia now supplies more than 60% of world nickel. Jakarta halved initial mining quotas and tightened environmental/pricing rules, but mid-year quota increases, ore imports and rising exchange stocks undermined the scarcity thesis. The balance improved from extreme surplus, but sub-$16,000/t remains the cleaner confirmation that supply discipline has failed; >$17,000/t needs inventory and ore-availability confirmation.

### Central-bank gold: structural bid survived a tactical drawdown

World Gold Council data showed 23 t of net official purchases in July and about 130 t year to date at that point; China's PBOC then reported roughly 20 t in August, its largest monthly purchase since 2023. This supports the medium-term floor but did not prevent September's 6.6% decline when real yields surged.

### OPEC+: quota policy is secondary to deliverability

OPEC+ kept October policy unchanged after completing the phased rollback of 1.65 mb/d of cuts in September, while another roughly 2 mb/d layer remains through year-end. Actual output remained well below headline capacity because Gulf disruptions constrained flows. The 4 October meeting is therefore a deliverability and 2027-baseline test, not just a quota headline.

### Platinum and silver: deficit stories diverged

WPIC reversed its 2026 platinum balance from a 297 koz deficit to a 265 koz surplus after H1 investment outflows and weaker jewellery demand, although above-ground stocks remain only about 3.4 months of demand. Silver Institute work still points to a sixth consecutive annual deficit, but high prices and weak investor timing make liquidity and positioning as important as the annual balance.

### China: tech/industrial strength, property and household weakness

September official manufacturing PMI returned to 50.1 and private manufacturing rose more strongly, but the official employment index remained 48.4 and non-manufacturing new orders 46.5. Targeted PBOC credit, housing subsidies and the two-month US-China truce extension reduce tail risk; they do not establish broad household/property reflation.

## Three medium-term macro scenarios

### Base — managed acute stagflation with duration disorder (45%)

Brent holds mostly $95–110, Gulf flows improve unevenly, the Fed pauses in October but retains a December hike bias, BOJ and RBA remain restrictive, and US10Y trades 5.00–5.50%. Nasdaq concentration survives while broader equities and ASX lag. Gold trades $4,050–4,350 and copper remains firm without broad China confirmation.

### Upside / inflationary — renewed physical escalation (30%)

Talks fail, exports/products deteriorate and Brent sustains above $110. US10Y moves above 5.50%, DXY above 102, gold initially weakens below $4,100 unless a haven override emerges, ASX breaks 8,650 and credit/breadth deteriorate. This is the path to restored broad disorderly risk-off.

### Downside / disinflationary — flow repair and weak labour (25%)

Gulf exports normalise, Brent falls below $95 then $90, US payrolls and CPI soften, the Fed pauses with lower path risk, and US10Y falls below 5.00%. Gold recovers above $4,300, equity breadth improves, DXY falls below 100.5 and AUD/USD retakes 0.710. A durable regime shift also requires China/property stabilisation or a clearly weaker US nominal-growth pulse.
