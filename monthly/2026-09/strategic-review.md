## What changed this month — one page

### Strategic verdict

**INTACT — managed acute energy and long-duration stagflation, with policy/AI/FX divergence; disorderly risk-off remains fractured.**

August did not deliver the cluster required to break the proposed August frame. It strengthened the evidence that the regime’s **core** and **expression** can move separately:

- **Core intact:** Brent ended near $90, TTF rose 22% to about €70, the IEA’s 3Q26 deficit estimate doubled to 1.8 mb/d, US10Y ended around 4.76%, US PCE was 3.7% y/y and Australian trimmed-mean CPI remained 3.6%.
- **Disorderly expression fractured:** S&P 500 +2.6%, Nasdaq +3.9%, ASX about +0.6%, DXY -0.5% and no verified credit cascade.
- **Policy leg reactivated:** weak July payrolls first drove September Fed hike odds below 50%; Warsh’s 28 August speech and renewed US–Iran attacks lifted them to about 64%.
- **Precious breadth returned:** gold +9.7% and silver about +14.7%, but the Warsh reversal showed the tactical inverse-rates relationship still works. Strategic official/investment demand and short-run real-yield pressure coexist.
- **China stayed two-speed:** August manufacturing PMI improved to 49.8 and high-tech/equipment activity exceeded 51, while services remained 49.0. Copper/lithium strength therefore does not establish broad domestic reflation.
- **Yen risk returned without an unwind:** USD/JPY ended near 159.8 after retracing more than half the July intervention move; AUD/JPY rose and equities gained.

### Defining narrative

**August was a tug-of-war between physical scarcity and financial concentration.** Weak US jobs and softer July CPI initially reduced hike risk, weakening DXY and lifting gold, silver and duration. Physical energy never repaired: TTF kept rising, Gulf exports remained impaired and the IEA tightened its deficit assessment. Nvidia then demonstrated that extraordinary AI cash flow could support cap-weighted equities through high yields. Warsh’s Jackson Hole speech and renewed US–Iran attacks closed the month by restoring the oil → inflation → policy → yields channel.

The result was not contradictory once the frame is separated by horizon:

- **Strategic horizon:** official gold demand, silver/platinum deficits, critical-mineral concentration, AI power demand and physical energy constraints remained supportive.
- **Tactical horizon:** real yields, DXY and central-bank repricing still control day-to-day gold, duration and FX.
- **Index horizon:** mega-cap AI earnings can offset macro stress without proving broad risk-on.

## Structural shifts during August

### Simandou: ramp is now an operating variable

Simandou is no longer a distant supply story. First commercial shipments began in late 2025 and the integrated system is ramping; full nameplate is up to 120 Mt/y. The September frame treats Simandou as a **grade, mill-mix and Australian-discount** mechanism before it becomes a simple aggregate-volume shock. [Rio Tinto](https://www.riotinto.com/en/news/stories/simandous-time-has-come)

### Indonesian nickel: policy restraint versus supplemental supply

Indonesia’s 2026 national ore quota was set around 260–270 Mt versus 379 Mt approved for 2025, and Weda Bay’s initial permit was cut sharply. But August price action and reports of supplemental allocations show why quota announcements are not enough. Confirmation requires actual ore output, local premiums, smelter utilisation and LME/ShFE inventories. Nickel fell 2.6% in August despite nominal restraint. [Reuters on Weda Bay](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/eramet-says-indonesia-nickel-permit-volume-slashed-2026-2026-02-11/) · [Reuters structural analysis](https://www.reuters.com/markets/nickel-market-plays-indonesias-numbers-game-2026-01-23/)

### Central-bank gold: official demand remains structural

WGC’s June update showed H1 leaders led by Poland (82 t), Uzbekistan (41 t), China (40 t) and Kazakhstan (27 t). Its 2026 survey found 89% of respondents expected global official gold reserves to rise and 45% expected their own holdings to increase. This supports strategic demand but does not invalidate tactical real-yield sensitivity. [WGC June statistics](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/08/central-bank-gold-statistics-june-2026) · [WGC survey](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/central-banks)

### OPEC+ versus physical disruption

OPEC+ scheduled a 188 kb/d September adjustment. The IEA simultaneously forecast 2026 demand down 1.6 mb/d but a 3Q deficit of 1.8 mb/d because route/product constraints reduced usable supply. OPEC’s demand forecast remained positive. The strategic point is not which forecast “wins”; it is that **the variance around physical access is larger than the quota increment**. [OPEC+](https://www.opec.org/pr-detail/1854611-2-august-2026.html) · [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-august-2026)

### Precious deficits and technology demand

- Silver is expected to record a sixth consecutive annual deficit in 2026, though PV thrifting means solar installations do not translate one-for-one into ounces. [Silver Institute](https://silverinstitute.org/global-silver-investment-to-remain-strong-in-2026-against-the-backdrop-of-a-sixth-consecutive-annual-market-deficit/)
- WPIC’s next quarterly update is 9 September; its standing forecast points to a fourth consecutive platinum deficit, with above-ground stock cover under three months by end-2026. [WPIC](https://platinuminvestment.com/events)
- Palladium’s supply concentration remains supportive near term, but WPIC expects transition to surplus from 2027 under its base case. [WPIC 2–5 year view](https://platinuminvestment.com/supply-and-demand/2-to-5-year-view)

### Critical-mineral concentration

The IEA’s 2026 outlook narrowed the projected 2035 copper supply gap from about 30% to 25%, but did not remove it. Lithium demand grows fastest through 2040, while present-cycle supply response remains much easier than for copper. Refining/downstream diversification continues to lag mining announcements. [IEA 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2026/executive-summary)

### Gas regionalisation

TTF +22% versus Henry Hub +5% is the month’s cleanest structural relationship. European low storage and impaired Gulf/Qatari LNG flows coexist with record US Lower-48 output. The global gas shock remains regional rather than uniform.

## Three medium-term macro scenarios

### Base — managed acute stagflation (50%)

**Conditions:** Brent $85–95, TTF €60–75, partial but incomplete flow improvement; central banks deliver zero or one additional hikes; US10Y 4.50–4.85; DXY 98.8–100.5; credit orderly; AI earnings remain strong.

**Transmission:**

- Gold/silver supported strategically but volatile with real yields.
- Energy elevated; TTF outperforms Henry Hub.
- Nasdaq/ASX can hold if earnings/resource breadth offsets rates.
- Copper firm on supply/AI-grid demand; iron ore/nickel remain China/policy constrained.
- USD/JPY 157–162; intervention/BOJ limits one-way carry.

**Confirmation:** prices remain within proposed zones; no broad credit/equity cascade; physical routes improve only slowly.

**Invalidation:** upside physical escalation or the full disinflation-repair cluster.

### Upside / inflationary — renewed physical escalation (30%)

**Conditions:** Brent >$100 or TTF >€75 with worsening Gulf/LNG/product flows; hike odds ≥75%; US10Y >4.85 and real >2.50.

**Transmission:**

- Fed/RBA/BOJ tightening pressure rises; DXY may strengthen, though intervention can distort.
- Gold/silver initially face real-yield pressure; a two-session rise with oil/DXY/yields above gold $4,650 establishes the haven override.
- Nasdaq/duration and broad consumption weaken; energy producers outperform, but resources are not uniformly bullish.
- AUD is two-sided: terms-of-trade support versus China/risk/imported inflation.
- Credit/breadth decide whether “acute” becomes “disorderly.”

**Confirmation:** physical export/refinery/LNG deterioration plus cross-asset rates/credit response.

**Invalidation:** repaired flows, Brent <85 and TTF <60.

### Downside / disinflationary — flow repair and weak labour (20%)

**Conditions:** Brent < $84 for two closes with route/refinery repair; TTF <€55; payroll/inflation weaken; hike odds <40%; US10Y <4.45 and real <2.25.

**Transmission:**

- DXY and yields fall; broad duration and equities repair if credit stays stable.
- Gold benefits from lower real yields but may lose some geopolitical premium; net direction depends on ETF/official demand.
- Energy/resource earnings weaken; copper can outperform iron ore if grid/structural demand persists.
- AUD/JPY rises if easing is benign; falls if weak data becomes credit/growth stress.

**Confirmation:** multi-session physical and policy cluster plus broad equity breadth.

**Invalidation:** renewed attacks, Brent >$92 / TTF >€65, or sticky CPI/central-bank re-hawkishness.
