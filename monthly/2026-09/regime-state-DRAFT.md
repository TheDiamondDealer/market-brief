# Proposed Regime State — September 2026

> **DRAFT — AWAITING HUMAN APPROVAL.** Prepared 1 September 2026 AEST. This file does not replace `regime-state.md`.

## Verdict

**INTACT — managed acute energy and long-duration stagflation, with policy/AI/FX divergence; disorderly risk-off remains fractured.**

This is an **intact** verdict versus the August proposed frame, not a return to July’s one-way disorderly expression. August confirmed both sides of the frame:

- **Stagflation core remained active:** Brent ended near $90, TTF rose 22% to about €70, the US 10-year ended around 4.76%, PCE was 3.7% y/y, Australian CPI was 3.5% y/y and September Fed hike pricing recovered to about 64%.
- **Broad liquidation remained absent:** S&P 500 rose 2.6%, Nasdaq 3.9%, ASX about 0.6%, DXY fell about 0.5% and credit stress was not verified.
- **Expression rotated:** weak US payrolls briefly weakened the policy leg; AI earnings and a soft dollar supported risk/precious assets; Warsh and renewed US–Iran attacks restored the oil → inflation → rates pressure at month-end.

The system is therefore acute but managed: energy and long yields are regime anchors, while equities, DXY and intervention-distorted yen signals stop the disorderly classification.

## What changed during August

1. **Energy moved sideways in crude but tightened in gas and products.** Brent was roughly flat month-on-month, yet TTF rose 22%. The IEA estimated a 1.8 mb/d 3Q26 deficit, July inventories down 69 mb and refinery throughput nearly 5 mb/d below a year earlier. Renewed 31 August attacks showed that route risk remains convex. [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-august-2026) · [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-08-31/)
2. **Fed pricing completed a round trip.** July payrolls pushed September hike odds below 50%; Warsh’s 28 August speech and the renewed oil shock lifted them to about 64%. [Fed](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents.htm)
3. **Gold and silver regained strategic sponsorship.** Gold rose 9.7% and silver about 14.7%, but the sharp Warsh reversal confirmed that the short-run rates channel remains active. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/gold-hits-near-two-week-low-fed-chiefs-hawkish-stance-2026-08-31/)
4. **AI earnings prevented a broad duration unwind.** Nvidia reported $96.2bn quarterly revenue, up 106% y/y; Nasdaq gained 3.9% despite high yields. [Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027)
5. **The yen intervention effect faded.** USD/JPY ended near 159.8 and the yen lost about 1.7% in August, but AUD/JPY rose and equities gained: still no carry unwind.
6. **China improved at the margin without broad reflation.** Manufacturing PMI rose to 49.8; services stayed at 49.0. Copper/lithium strengthened while domestic demand and Dalian iron ore remained weak. [Reuters/NBS](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-factory-activity-improves-stays-contraction-august-2026-08-31/)
7. **Australia’s policy risk reopened.** The RBA held 4.35%, but July CPI at 3.5% y/y and trimmed mean 3.6% lifted market pricing for a 29 September hike to roughly a coin flip. [RBA](https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-19.html) · [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation)

## Active transmission map

1. **Route/refinery/LNG stress → energy prices and product availability → inflation expectations → Fed/RBA/BOJ reaction → nominal/real yields → precious metals and duration.**
2. **Investment/fiscal term premium → long yields higher even when DXY is below 100.**
3. **AI cash-flow concentration → cap-weighted equity resilience despite high real yields.** Breadth, semiconductors and credit decide whether it generalises.
4. **Fed–BOJ differential + intervention policy → USD/JPY.** Intervention-driven yen strength is not a carry unwind; AUD/JPY and credit are confirmation tools.
5. **China export/high-tech strength + domestic-demand weakness → copper/lithium can diverge from iron ore and services.**
6. **Supply concentration → nonlinear commodities:** Simandou high-grade ore, Indonesian nickel quotas, uranium origins, PGM supply and Gulf energy routes.

## Formal flip tests

### Re-strengthen into disorderly stagflation

Require **two or more** sustained for two closes, with at least one physical or credit confirmation:

- Brent >$95 with deteriorating Gulf flows/product markets, or >$100 on any physically confirmed escalation.
- TTF >€75 with worsening LNG arrivals/storage.
- US10Y >4.85% and real 10Y >2.50%.
- September Fed hike probability ≥75%.
- DXY >101.5.
- USD/JPY >162 without effective intervention.
- Nasdaq <25,800 with weak breadth/semiconductors and wider credit.

### Stabilise inside managed stagflation

- Brent $85–92 and TTF €60–72 with improving flows;
- US10Y 4.50–4.80 and DXY 99–100.5;
- hike odds 40–70%;
- USD/JPY 157–161 without cross-asset deleveraging;
- Nasdaq 25,800–26,800 with orderly credit.

### Break toward disinflation / broad repair

Require the cluster, not one data point:

- Brent < $84 for two closes **and** verified route/refinery repair;
- TTF < €55 with improving LNG arrivals/storage;
- US10Y <4.45 and real 10Y <2.25;
- Fed hike odds <40%;
- DXY <99 without funding stress;
- Nasdaq >26,800 with broad/equal-weight participation and stable credit.

### Break toward liquidity dislocation

- USD/JPY <156 and AUD/JPY <110 **with** broad equity losses and wider credit;
- simultaneous liquidation in gold, copper and equities;
- funding-basis stress or emergency central-bank liquidity measures;
- impaired market functioning.
