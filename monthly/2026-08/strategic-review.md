## What changed this month — one page

**Proposed regime:** **SHIFTING — acute energy/long-duration stagflation remains intact, but the disorderly risk-off expression has fractured.**

1. **Energy became the month’s dominant factor.** Brent rose about 24% to $90.12 and WTI about 21% to $84.67. Route impairment and record product cracks confirm that this was more than a futures risk premium. OPEC+’s 188 kb/d September increase is helpful at the margin, not a reset.
2. **The policy cluster stayed restrictive.** Fed held 3.50–3.75% with three hike dissents; RBA remained 4.35%; BOJ remained 1.0% with an upside-inflation bias.
3. **The dollar/yen expression broke.** DXY fell below 100 and reported coordinated intervention pushed USD/JPY below 160. Intervention-driven yen strength is not a confirmed carry unwind.
4. **US equity stress became concentrated rather than systemic.** Nasdaq lost about 3.2% in July but Microsoft/Amazon drove a late cap-weighted recovery. S&P 500 was roughly flat; no credit cascade was confirmed.
5. **Gold’s sign remains unresolved.** It gained 1.1% in July but ended at $4,049.83, on the downside trigger. High real yields and central-bank/geopolitical demand are pulling in opposite directions.
6. **China’s aggregate signal weakened.** July manufacturing PMI 49.2 and non-manufacturing 49.0 reinforce weak activity, but copper/supply policy prevent a simple “China down, all metals down” rule.
7. **Structural supply became more concrete.** Simandou shipped into China and guides to 5–10 Mt of 2026 sales; Indonesia indicated 250–260 Mt of 2026 nickel quotas; central banks reported 41 t of net gold purchases in May.
8. **Proposed trigger reset:** gold $4,050; Brent $92 / acute $100 / exit $84; ASX 9,000 up and 8,800 down; September Fed hike odds 75% / invalidation 50%; USD/JPY 162 stress and 155 carry-unwind test; US10Y 4.75%; Nasdaq 25,000.
9. **Base case:** managed acute stagflation—energy remains constrained but no broad liquidity break. Upside-inflation risk is route/product re-escalation. Downside-disinflation needs physical repair, not just cheaper futures.
10. **Highest-stakes August event:** RBA decision and Statement on Monetary Policy on 11 August for the Australia/AUD/ASX transmission; globally, 12 August US CPI is the cleanest test of the energy-to-core-policy chain.

## Structural shifts

### Simandou

Rio Tinto confirms first shipment in December 2025, first full shipment delivered to China, and 5–10 Mt of 2026 sales guidance on a 100% basis. The SimFer system targets a 30-month ramp to 60 Mtpa; combined project capacity can reach 120 Mtpa. This is now a measurable ramp and a medium-term high-grade supply shock, not a conceptual future project ([Rio Tinto Q1](https://www.riotinto.com/en/news/releases/2026/rio-tinto-releases-first-quarter-2026-production-results)).

### Indonesian nickel

Indonesia indicated 250–260 Mt of 2026 quota, down sharply from 379 Mt of 2025 allocations, while allowing potential exemptions for domestic smelter shortages. Treat the number as policy intent; ore premiums, actual mining and smelter utilisation are the confirmation ([ANTARA](https://en.antaranews.com/news/422261/indonesia-limits-nickel-quota-expansion-to-support-global-prices)).

### Central-bank gold

Reported net central-bank purchases were 41 t in May; Poland bought 18 t and China 10 t. WGC’s survey found 89% expect global official holdings to rise and 45% plan to raise their own holdings. This is a durable support mechanism, not proof that gold ignores real yields every session ([WGC statistics](https://www.gold.org/index.php/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/07/central-bank-gold-statistics-central-banks-remain-committed-gold); [WGC survey](https://www.gold.org/news-and-events/press-releases/central-banks-set-step-gold-buying-over-next-year)).

### OPEC+ and energy balance

Seven core OPEC+ members agreed to raise September quotas by 188 kb/d, finishing the unwind of 1.65 mb/d voluntary 2023 cuts. Roughly 2 mb/d of 2022 cuts remain through year-end and the group meets again 6 September. Physical route/product stress means paper supply increases may not translate one-for-one to delivered barrels ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/opec-has-agreement-principle-september-quota-increase-pause-thereafter-source-2026-08-02/)).

### Critical-mineral concentration

The IEA says energy-mineral demand grew roughly 10% annually over the past two years and lithium about 25%; investment fell 9% in 2025, with battery-metal investment down 20%. Copper’s projected 2035 gap remains around 25% under current projects. Structural scarcity is real, but commodity-specific supply response means it is not a blanket bullish basket ([IEA 2026](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-critical-minerals-outlook-2026/executive-summary)).

## Three macro scenarios

### Base — managed acute stagflation (50%)

**Transmission:** Brent $84–95; product markets remain tight; Fed holds with hike risk; US10Y 4.45–4.80; DXY 99–101; USD/JPY 155–160; equities volatile but credit orderly.

**Confirmation:** physical energy stress persists without new route closure; CPI stays sticky; no broad equity/credit cascade.

**Invalidation:** physical repair plus Brent <84 and yields <4.45, or acute re-escalation above $100 with cross-asset stress.

### Upside inflation — renewed physical escalation (30%)

**Transmission:** Brent >$100 and product cracks remain extreme → inflation expectations and long yields rise → Fed/RBA hike pricing rises → duration, gold under base-case yield pressure, USD/JPY stress returns.

**Confirmation:** route/export/refinery deterioration; US10Y >4.75 and real >2.50; Fed hike odds ≥75%; broad cyclicals weaken.

**Invalidation:** repaired flows, Brent <84 and product cracks normalise.

### Downside disinflation — physical repair and policy repricing (20%)

**Transmission:** flows recover and Brent falls below $84 → inflation compensation/yields fall → hike odds <50% → duration and broad equities repair; gold response depends on real yields versus risk premium.

**Confirmation:** official/transit evidence, two Brent closes <84, US10Y <4.45, DXY <99 without funding stress, Nasdaq >26,400 with breadth.

**Invalidation:** renewed route attack, Brent >92, or sticky CPI/hawkish central-bank repricing.
